# AI Behaviour Framework

## Goal
Provide deterministic enemy decision logic in simulation layer using the same input contract as human players.

## Source files
- AI policy:
  - `apps/game-web/src/sim/ai.ts`
- Tests:
  - `apps/game-web/src/sim/ai.test.ts`

## Design
- AI logic runs in `src/sim` and has no renderer/DOM dependencies.
- `tickAiController(...)` outputs `PlayerFrameInput` directly.
- `buildFrameInputWithAi(...)` combines local human input with AI input using standard `FrameInput` shape.

## Determinism
- AI controller keeps explicit RNG state.
- Experimental post-event posture rolls use a separate deterministic RNG stream. Testing a recovery or clash posture therefore does not consume the ordinary maneuver, mistake, defense, or attack-choice stream merely because the experiment needs extra random samples.
- Movement memory is explicit controller state: super boost has a deterministic recommit delay and may start at most twice before a launch, special, dunk, parry, or launch break resets the approach budget. Launch breaks receive a seeded per-launch hold/spend decision and delay, with stronger reserve bias for the last break. Only a low-fuel outward boundary emergency or committed finishing dunk bypasses that plan, and imminent natural recovery conserves the resource. This prevents both super boost and launch break from becoming automatic tactical loops while retaining ordinary boost as the fallback approach tool.
- RNG progression uses shared deterministic RNG utilities from `src/sim/rng.ts`.
- Fixed-seed fixed-step simulation test verifies repeated runs produce identical action timelines and end state values.

## Post-event policy experiments

The local batch runner exposes three independent policy dimensions. `--recovery-policy` controls only the first decision after launch control returns:

- `legacy`: current shipped behavior; no added post-recovery posture.
- `spacing`: briefly retreats or orbits with ordinary movement before another offensive decision.
- `evasive`: uses the spacing posture and may spend super boost away from the opponent at close range.

`--clash-policy` controls only the first decision after a simultaneous launch clash:

- `legacy`: current shipped behavior; the normal decision policy resumes immediately.
- `spacing`: briefly retreats or orbits with ordinary movement before another offensive decision. It never spends super boost through the recovery-policy escape rule.

`--pursuit-policy` controls re-engagement after actual pressure has exited into mid/long range:

- `legacy`: current shipped pursuit behavior resumes normally.
- `neutral_hold`: after pressure exits, or after a safe close control return outside pressure, waits until both fighters next have neutral control and briefly preserves roughly 30 units of spacing through orbit and light range correction. It does not replace a launch chase, punish, projectile response, boundary escape, or other non-neutral state.

## Designer behavior tuning

The local Balance Lab exposes a versioned `gw.ai-behavior-tuning.v15` layer for smaller, one-variable experiments without editing controller source. It can scale engagement distance, neutral inward drive, reaction delay, error rate, and risk appetite; postpone ordinary auto-lock boost; add exact post-exit, post-clash, or post-recovery spacing frames; add an offense-only read after an AI's own commitment and authored recovery; create a close-control-return bait/read window that steers outward, withholds proactive launch and package-declared movement dash, and still permits defensive or reactive counter-launch responses; intercept the returning fighter's first inward locomotion as a zero-default counterstep probe; briefly suppress inward ordinary Boost pressure and package-declared movement-dash specials once both fighters are action-ready after natural launch recovery or launch break; scale a repeated package-declared movement-dash first choice after a close control return; give the attacker a short offense-only respect window when the opponent regains control; test a one-shot weighted orbit-or-retreat choice after the fighter regains control; test a separate one-shot weighted reposition after the fighter's own strike and authored recovery; test zero-default complementary initiative/response roles with a shared reset phase; stage a post-control defense window for a fighter with slower authored launch startup; test an available block special before a threat or parry only after a nearby launch or incoming projectile that parry can actually stop; reserve an authored block special for one timing-valid response roll when an opponent starts a nearby launch; change seeded retreat/escape posture; scale how much authored dunk-startup movement the AI may count when pursuing a helpless zero-fuel target; and scale launch, special, dunk, parry, or launch-break decision weights. Finish pursuit ships at `0.70`; older v5/v6 replays and drafts migrate to their historical `0.25` behavior, v7 evidence receives a neutral zero-frame steering value, v8 evidence receives neutral opponent-recovery respect, v9 evidence receives a neutral post-commitment read value, v10 evidence receives zero tactical-reposition weight, v11 evidence receives a zero counterstep value, v12 evidence receives a zero post-control chase lock, v13 evidence receives a neutral repeat-dash weight of `1`, and v14 evidence receives zero between-exchange reposition weight.

General multiplicative scales, including `postControlRepeatDashWeightScale`, default to `1`; the post-commitment read scale, tactical-reposition weights, post-control counterstep, post-control chase lock, offsets, spacing, steering, opponent-recovery respect and commitment windows, recovery escape chance, all three post-control defense values, and committed-launch Guard chance default to `0`. The neutral object selects the shipped input policy, while the controller's `flow-v25` identity prevents comparison with reports from older controller behavior. Tuning is sanitized, fingerprinted, frozen when a local AI-vs-AI round starts, included in draft/experiment/telemetry/batch artifacts, and passed to both deterministic controllers. It never changes simulator move data and is not selected for human, arcade, online, or ranked play.

The v12 **Post-Control Counterstep Scale** probe arms only after genuine control return at close range. On the returning fighter's first inward movement, boost, super boost, or tactical request, it clears inward locomotion while preserving launch, parry, and authored Guard choices; threats, openings, finish opportunities, boundary emergencies, and renewed control loss cancel it. Decision-flow telemetry records activation windows and active seconds separately from tactical repositioning. A mirrored four-set Cadet/Veteran screen rejected `0 -> 1`: blocked Chase rounds increased from `4/36` to `8/34`, Veteran average set length rose from `120.39s` to `144.79s`, and Veteran Vanguard completed only `1/6` finish starts. The shipped value remains zero. The control is retained only as a deterministic human-review probe, not a recommended policy.

The separate global **Combat Boost Lock Delay** probe tested the narrower mechanics hypothesis that free ordinary boost erases authored attack and recovery commitment. Any positive value cancels ordinary boost at launch, dunk, special, parry, or launch break, pauses through interruption and control loss, prevents reacquisition until the configured delay has elapsed after control returns, and leaves ordinary steering and paid super boost intact. Decision-flow v3 counts complete lock frames, held-input frames, and cancellations. An early winner-stopping screen rejected `0.18s`, but its baseline/candidate round membership was not suitable for causal measurement. The corrected batch-v20 screen tested the minimum one-frame value on identical 48-round Cadet/Veteran mirrored samples. Chase moved from `4 Blocked / 44 Watch / 0 Observed` to `18 Blocked / 26 Watch / 4 Observed`; Commitment moved from `0 Blocked / 38 Watch / 10 Observed` to `12 Blocked / 22 Watch / 14 Observed`. The small reduction in total flagged ratios did not offset the large increase in severe failures. This confirms that blanket combat cancellation can redistribute or deepen the loop rather than create agency. Live behavior remains at the zero/off value.

The v13 **Post-Control Chase Lock Frames** probe isolates the boost-first moment after natural launch recovery or launch break without changing simulator physics; stun, parry, command-grab, projectile, and dunk-recovery transitions do not arm it. It arms only at close range when there is no finish, boundary, projectile, or helpless-target emergency, then waits until both fighters are genuinely action-ready before starting its timer. While active it preserves steering and outward low-fuel escape, along with launch, Guard, parry, projectiles, dunk, and paid Super Boost, but suppresses inward ordinary Boost pressure and any special whose character package declares `special.movement_dash.v1`; a tactical action or paid Super Boost consumes the window. Decision-flow v4 separates opportunity windows, active time, Boost-suppressed frames, otherwise-ready movement-dash time, and consumption. The launch-only `flow-v21` matched Veteran screen rejected `6`, `12`, and `18` frames. Six frames retained both Blocked Chase samples and introduced four Blocked Commitment samples. Twelve frames doubled Blocked Chase from two to four. Eighteen retained two Blocked Chase samples, introduced two Blocked Commitment samples, raised shared-pressure occupancy `7.3` points, reduced neutral resets by `0.51` per round and exchange resets by `2.6` points, and raised brief exits by `2.68` points. Immediate re-launch again split by package: Duelist fell `15.1` points while Vanguard rose `7.3` points. No timeout regression occurred, and representative baseline and candidate replays reproduced their checksums. The value remains zero and the control is retained only for deterministic human review.

The v14 **Package-Declared Movement-Dash Repeat Weight After Control Return** control is a local, neutral-default experiment. Its default `1` preserves existing package behavior. A lower value only scales a repeated `special.movement_dash.v1` candidate when movement dash would be the first tactical choice after a close control return. It does not lock movement, actions, or defense and does not change simulator physics. It cannot be promoted without identical-seed flow evidence and replay review; no passing evidence is claimed.

Its fast-screen bar is flow-first and fixed before the full alpha matrix: the candidate must have non-zero weighted opportunities and reduce repeated movement-dash selections where exposed; it may not add a Blocked Commitment, Separation, or Chase sample, increase timeout share, raise pressure occupancy by more than two percentage points, reduce shared action-ready share by more than one point, or erase an existing qualified finish completion. Sustained reset after control return or after the first accepted action must improve without increasing the returning fighter's share of closure. A candidate that only substitutes launch, extends the set, or improves one control-return ratio while worsening a loop stage is rejected before the larger run. Win percentage is not part of this bar.

The first strict screen rejected promotion. A `1 -> 0.25` three-round mechanism smoke exposed four weighted windows but still selected movement dash in all four and reproduced the baseline exactly. The stronger `1 -> 0` 48-round Cadet/Veteran mirrored screen exposed 46 windows over 148 weighted frames, consumed all 46, and selected no repeated dash. Duelist's first accepted post-control choices broadened from `154` Special, `34` Launch, and `4` Parry to `108` Special, `48` Launch, `24` Boost, `4` Super Boost, and `6` Parry. Sustained reset after a pressure-range return improved from `10/106` to `26/114`, and after the first pressure action from `32/136` to `52/148`; timeouts fell from eight to four, Blocked Chase from 12 to 8, and Blocked Separation from 2 to 0. However, Commitment regressed from `0 Blocked / 38 Watch / 10 Observed` to `2 / 38 / 8`, neutral resets fell by `0.47` per round on average, exchange-reset ratio fell `2.15` points, and dominant-action share rose `0.95` points. A checksum-valid candidate trace showed the zeroed repeat dash resolving directly to Launch at `26.8s` and `33.0s`. The candidate therefore fails the predeclared complete-loop bar; the default remains `1`, the control remains local-only, and no full alpha matrix was run.

`flow-v23` corrected a narrower experiment defect: an explicitly enabled neutral hold can now arm after a safe close control return outside pressure, rather than requiring the fighters first to cross the pressure boundary. The current 144-round Cadet/Veteran alpha baseline still failed at `16 Blocked / 114 Watch / 14 Observed` Commitment and `30 / 114 / 0` Chase. A six-frame hold improved those counts to `10 / 108 / 26` and `20 / 124 / 0`, but every reached Chase sample remained flagged and the control-return reset gate remained below its `0.20` requirement. The candidate therefore was not promoted; `neutralHoldFrames` remains `0` and every shipped pursuit policy remains `legacy`.

`flow-v24` narrows the zero-default steering experiment into **Post-Control Bait / Read Frames**. While active, the returning fighter keeps outward/orbit steering and may still parry, Guard, evade projectiles, pursue a finish, escape a boundary emergency, or counter-launch a launch that is already starting; it does not proactively launch or use a package-declared movement dash. On the matched 48-round Cadet/Veteran screen, the baseline recorded `0/38/10` Commitment and `6/42/0` Chase, `23.0%` shared action-ready share, `20.4%` pressure-range control-return reset conversion, 146 re-launches within one second, and no timeouts. Four, six, and eight bait frames all failed the complete-loop bar. Six frames cut immediate re-launches to 96 and produced four Observed Chase rounds, but worsened Commitment to `6/40/2`, reduced action-ready share to `16.4%`, and reduced control-return reset conversion to `15.0%`. A deliberate paired screen added 4/6/8 attacker observe frames; the least severe 6/4 combination lowered Blocked Chase from six to four but introduced six Blocked Commitment rounds, raised pressure during shared-ready time from `49.8%` to `56.8%`, and reduced reset conversion to `15.8%`. Longer paired windows were worse. Every involved value remains zero, no full alpha matrix was run for a failed fast-screen candidate, and the artifacts are rejection evidence rather than a recommendation.

`flow-v25` adds **Between-Exchange Reposition Weight** as a separate zero-default one-shot choice after the AI's own launch, dunk, projectile, or command-grab strike ends. The opportunity survives authored end lag and an opponent's overlapping action, but cannot select while the opponent is committed or open; punish, defense, finish, projectile, boundary, and renewed-control-loss states retain priority. A neutral continue-moving outcome makes the value a real weight rather than a guaranteed choice when attacks are recovering. The matched 48-round Cadet/Veteran screen rejected `0.5`, `1`, and `2`. Baseline Commitment/Separation/Chase was `0/38/10`, `2/4/42`, and `6/42/0` (Blocked/Watch/Observed), with `23.0%` shared action-ready share and 146 one-second re-launches. Every candidate increased blocked Separation to six and blocked Chase to ten; `0.5` and `1` also introduced two blocked Commitment rounds, while shared action-ready share fell to `20.5-21.2%`. All 48 finishes still completed and no timeouts occurred, but those preserved outcomes do not offset the loop regressions. The default remains zero and the artifacts are rejection evidence, not a recommendation.

A proposed `flow-v26` paired-counterstep variant briefly armed the same movement-only response for the pursuer when the opponent regained control, without suppressing package-declared movement-dash actions. It was screened at scales `0.25`, `0.5`, and `1` against the same mirrored 48-round baseline. Every value introduced new Blocked Commitment samples; the full-strength candidate also worsened Separation from `2` to `4` Blocked rounds and Chase from `6` to `8` Blocked rounds. Shorter values still left Chase blocked and worsened Commitment. The variant was rejected in full, its source changes were removed, and the controller identity remains `flow-v25`.

A later ordinary-movement sidestep probe reused the accepted ordinary-Boost collision-lane geometry and withheld proactive attacks for 12 frames after a newly committed Boost. On an identical-seed 144-round Cadet/Veteran Vanguard/Duelist screen, Blocked Commitment improved from `18` to `14`, but Blocked Chase worsened from `26` to `32`, Separation watch samples rose from `8` to `14`, and average set time rose from `168.78s` to `175.78s`. Across `1,338` accepted counterplay opportunities it produced no clean pass and only four avoided-but-closed outcomes. Ordinary movement acceleration cannot clear the observed `0.13-0.26s` approach windows reliably, so the control and its v16/`flow-v27` draft were rejected and removed. The shared geometry extraction remains behavior-neutral; live AI remains v15/`flow-v25`.

A separate short-lived **Incoming Committed Boost Evasion** prototype tested the intended counterplay where a fighter reads a newly committed ordinary Boost and spends fuel on a short lateral Super Boost. It was screened on the identical 48-round Cadet/Veteran mirrored sample before receiving a tuning-schema field. At a `0.25` response chance, Blocked Separation improved from `2` to `0`, but Blocked Chase worsened from `12` to `14`, Commitment regressed from `0 Blocked / 38 Watch / 10 Observed` to `6 / 34 / 8`, and timeouts rose from `8` to `10`. Super Boost starts increased from `880` to `968` while shared-pressure occupancy rose from `48.7%` to `50.0%`. The candidate failed the predeclared complete-loop stop condition, so the stronger `0.50` screen was not run, all source changes were removed, and no schema, package, policy revision, or live default changed.

The same idea was retested after **Ordinary Boost startup V1** created a real `0.12s` reaction window. Deterministic `0.10`, `0.25`, and `0.50` response chances were screened against a fresh `0.12s` baseline. The 48-round `0.50` sample initially looked promising, reducing Blocked Chase from `10` to `4` with no timeout or other blocked-stage regression and producing four clean Boost-line passes. It did not generalise. On the 144-round Cadet/Veteran matrix, `0.50` moved Neutral from `0 Blocked / 0 Watch / 144 Observed` to `0 / 2 / 142`, Separation from `0 / 12 / 132` to `0 / 20 / 124`, and Chase from `26 / 104 / 14` to `22 / 110 / 12`. It produced eight clean passes and raised stable evasions from `22` to `28`, but total Chase issues increased, physical Boost contact rose from `270` to `280`, and the extra fuel spend did not create durable neutral. The retest was rejected, its controller/schema/editor changes were removed, and v15/`flow-v25` remains live. Local rejection artifacts are `boost-acceleration-012-evasion-baseline-144.json` and `boost-acceleration-012-evasion-050-144.json`.

Character package v1 now has an optional `ai` pacing block, defaulting to approach multiplier `1`, ordinary-Boost distance offset `0`, and post-control spacing frames `0`. The class-specific spacing window reuses the threat-safe outward/orbit read after that character regains control within 36 units; it does not change human physics or suppress emergency defense and reactive counters. Effective values compose with the global local-AI controls, are available as per-character Balance Lab fields, and are included in registry, draft, replay, and batch fingerprints. Fixed-seed one-variable screens rejected Vanguard approach `0.75` and Boost offset `8`: the first worsened Blocked Chase from `12` to `20`; the second reduced it only to `10` while introducing eight Blocked Commitment samples. Duelist spacing at one, two, and four frames also failed the complete-loop bar. Four frames improved Cadet Chase but worsened Veteran Commitment/Chase; two frames reduced aggregate Blocked Chase from `12` to `8` while creating six Blocked Commitment samples; one frame retained all `12` Blocked Chase samples while creating four Blocked Commitment samples. Shipping packages therefore retain neutral AI values.

The separate global **Committed Boost/Dash Input Authority** control tests whether auto-target commitment itself prevents retreat and side-step play. `0` preserves the existing target lock. Values up to `1` blend normalized held movement into ordinary Boost and package-declared `special.movement_dash.v1` direction when commitment starts, then latch that direction for the move; zero movement input still targets the opponent. The value is deterministic, snapshot/replay compatible, and defaults to `0`. Identical-seed 48-round screens rejected `0.25`, `0.50`, and `1.00`: the baseline had eight timeouts, while the candidates had 16, 10, and 20, and every candidate introduced Blocked Commitment samples. The `0.50` candidate also raised shared-pressure occupancy from `48.9%` to `62.2%`. The control remains a manual movement probe and no live input behavior changed.

The browser's gameplay-loop diagnostics link directly to these controls when the evidence indicates pursuit, commitment, or post-event policy. A point-blank pursuit warning therefore points to engagement, inward-drive, boost-distance, hold, and retreat behavior before collision impulse; a carried brief re-entry chooses controls from its recorded cause; narrow or repetitive commitments point to decision weights before move strength. **Neutral Inward Drive Scale** transfers reduced radial approach into orbit input rather than slowing the fighter, while **Neutral Boost Distance Offset** delays only the ordinary free auto-lock boost threshold. It does not alter super boost, finish chase, low-fuel chase, move data, or collision rules.

## Designer controller roles

Balance Lab can replace either local AI-vs-AI controller with a deterministic test role without changing simulator mechanics. **Passive Dummy** remains neutral and never launch-breaks, **Defense Dummy** remains stationary but parries nearby commitments and immediately launch-breaks, and **Escape Dummy** retreats or steers inward near the boundary, boosts under pressure, and launch-breaks. **Adaptive** is the normal full controller.

The adaptive controller still advances its deterministic state underneath a scripted role, so switching roles does not introduce an unrelated random-stream implementation. The selected per-side roles are frozen at round reset, shown in decision traces and replay labels, and included in scenario and telemetry fingerprints. The feature is local-only and does not enter arcade, matchmaking, rollback, online, or ranked input generation.

The Balance Lab **Gameplay probe** selector packages common role/scenario combinations around explicit questions: full-loop rhythm, offense into a passive target, commitment into defense, launch-break agency, pursuit against escape, post-clash reset, AI or human post-control agency, and zero-fuel finish. A direct scenario or role edit becomes a Custom setup. This keeps a manual test repeatable without turning its result into an automatic class score.

Run the same seed matrix locally without changing source:

```bash
cd apps/game-web
npx tsx scripts/ai-matchup-batch.ts --games 8 --max-round-seconds 60 --difficulty "cadet,veteran" --characters "vanguard,duelist" --recovery-policy legacy --report-name policy-legacy
npx tsx scripts/ai-matchup-batch.ts --games 8 --max-round-seconds 60 --difficulty "cadet,veteran" --characters "vanguard,duelist" --recovery-policy spacing --report-name policy-spacing
npx tsx scripts/ai-matchup-batch.ts --games 8 --max-round-seconds 60 --difficulty "cadet,veteran" --characters "vanguard,duelist" --recovery-policy evasive --report-name policy-evasive
npx tsx scripts/ai-matchup-batch.ts --games 8 --max-round-seconds 60 --difficulty "cadet,veteran" --characters "vanguard,duelist" --recovery-policy legacy --clash-policy spacing --report-name clash-spacing
npx tsx scripts/ai-matchup-batch.ts --games 8 --max-round-seconds 60 --difficulty "cadet,veteran" --characters "vanguard,duelist" --recovery-policy legacy --clash-policy legacy --pursuit-policy neutral_hold --report-name pursuit-neutral-hold
```

All three defaults remain `legacy`. In the first eight-set-per-direction recovery screen, `evasive` reduced aggregate contact from `11.2%` to `10.6%`, immediate re-launch after control return from `26.9%` to `23.4%`, launch hits per round from `15.01` to `14.46`, and average helpless share from `29.7%` to `28.4%`. It also increased timeout share from `25.3%` to `28.1%`, and the direction-level results were inconsistent. Recovery `spacing` increased first-action delay without reducing aggregate immediate re-launch. Neither recovery experiment was promoted.

An isolated eight-set-per-direction clash screen held recovery at `legacy`. Clash `spacing` removed observed repeat clashes within one second and moved average first accepted post-clash action from about `0.20s` to `0.57s`; rapid launch recommit fell from `16.7%/19.0%` to `10.6%/2.1%` for P1/P2. It did not solve the loop: physical contact stayed at `11.2%`, immediate re-launch after control slightly increased, and timeout share rose from `25.3%` to `32.6%`. It remains an opt-in diagnostic policy rather than shipped behavior. This result indicates that ordinary pursuit after many kinds of separation, not clash recurrence alone, is the stronger next AI-design target.

An eight-set-per-direction pursuit screen then held recovery and clash at `legacy`. With same-frame event ordering and causal transition context, `neutral_hold` reduced aggregate timeout share from `25.3%` to `22.4%`, physical contact from `11.18%` to `10.98%`, and carried brief re-entry only from `15.40%` to `14.86%`; overall approach intent remained effectively unchanged at `84.50%` versus `84.22%`. Direction-level timeout and carried-re-entry results were mixed, so it was not promoted. The stronger finding is controller-specific: `92/122` legacy carried re-entries were held approach and `21/122` were held boost, while only one was action-recovery momentum and none were residual velocity. Pursuit and commitment policy must therefore be inspected before recovery or separation physics.

A first designer-tuning screen tested one control at a time on the same eight-set-per-direction matrix. `engagementDistanceScale=2` reduced physical contact from `11.18%` to `4.14%`, but raised pressure occupancy from `56.83%` to `82.56%`, reduced reset success from `54.60%` to `29.30%`, and timed out `153/160` rounds. It was rejected: the fighters were no longer colliding as often, but they remained in non-progressing pressure. `neutralHoldFrames=18` was much less disruptive and moved reset success to `57.25%`, while pressure, contact, carried re-entry, and timeout behavior stayed close to baseline. That small mixed effect was not promoted either. These results reinforce that the target is a readable neutral-to-finish loop, not minimum contact or a desired class win percentage.

A later eight-game-per-direction, cadet-and-veteran fixed-seed screen isolated neutral pursuit from ordinary boost commitment. Reducing Neutral Inward Drive Scale to `0.5` increased pressure occupancy from `55.9%` to `60.6%` and introduced a `2.8%` timeout share; combined `0.5`/`16` and `0.25`/`30` inward-drive/boost-offset candidates kept pressure at `60.6-61.5%` and shifted the repetitive loop into longer special-heavy mid-range exchanges. Those inward-drive candidates were rejected. Holding inward drive at `1` and applying only a `16`-unit Neutral Boost Distance Offset reduced physical contact from `11.1%` to `9.9%`, held pressure near baseline at `55.3%`, retained a zero timeout share, and reduced accepted combat starts from `224.0` to `215.1` per minute. It remains a visual-review candidate rather than a shipped default because lower cadence alone does not prove a more readable match.

The v6 commitment screen was also rejected. Early independent observe/press/reset timers reduced some body contact but drove pressure occupancy as high as `75-92%`, repeated specials, and created widespread timeouts or draws. A `flow-v12` follow-up made the experiment complementary: both controllers derive one initiative owner from the round seed, agree on ownership changes after a clash, offensive whiff, or control return, and expire unanswered roles back to legacy behavior. Guard and movement-dash utility specials no longer masquerade as offensive whiffs, while authored Guard remains a low-weight close read and a stronger real-threat response. Even with those corrections, a matched 24-frame response, 18-frame initiative, and 36-frame reset candidate raised timeout share from `36.0%` to `88.3%`, pressure from `56.4%` to `73.5%`, and specials from `24.7` to `42.6` per round while reset conversion fell from `63.2%` to `20.1%`. Immediate re-launch fell from `27.8%` to `1.7%`, demonstrating why that metric cannot be optimized alone. All three commitment values remain zero-default investigation controls.

A 12-set-per-direction Veteran screen first tested the v3 **Post-Control Guard Chance** at `0.25` and `0.50` without forced movement. Both were rejected because an immediate anticipatory parry created another punish window rather than agency. Version 4 removes that control and instead separates defense-window duration, block-special chance, and threat-confirmed parry chance. The window is available only to the close-range fighter with slower authored launch startup, suppresses an ordinary launch choice, and consumes at most one deterministic threat-parry roll.

The v4 work also found and fixed three sources of false directional evidence: deliberate AI mistakes used a fixed world-space vector, one seat could move and spend fuel before a same-frame opposing hit, and the second combat resolver could observe launch/special phase changes that the first could not. AI mistake drift is now opponent-relative; existing combat resolves before either locomotion phase; specials, launches, and dunks have explicit shared action phases; and launch clash eligibility uses only frame-start state. A complete mirrored-state trace plus all 12 batch seeds across all five round indices now require swapped seats to produce mirrored state, frame count, and winner.

After that correction, a matched v4 candidate with a 12-frame window, `0.50` block-special chance, and `1.00` threat-parry chance was rejected. It raised pressure by `0.8` percentage points and physical contact by `0.6`, reduced neutral resets by `1.35` per round, raised brief exits by `2.57` points, and reduced Vanguard's sustained control-return reset conversion by `15.1` points. All three controls therefore remain zero-default inspection tools rather than shipped behavior.

A later threat audit found that ordinary defense treated every nearby committed action as parryable even though simulation parry only interrupts launches and projectiles. Dunk and Duelist's movement-only Pressure Dash no longer trigger that impossible read. Vanguard's authored Guard remains separately allowed to anticipate a movement follow-up because its delayed active window can cover the actual attack. This changed AI decision validity rather than move strength.

The v7 finish screen then isolated finish-only pursuit reach from package-authored dunk movement. Raising **Finish Pursuit Reach Scale** from the historical `0.25` to `0.70` converted four exact formerly missed Cadet/Veteran replay cases with matching per-frame checksums and reduced timeout share by roughly 21-23 percentage points in a larger matched screen without materially changing contact or pressure. Vanguard package candidates at startup pursuit `62` and `66` were rejected because starts increased but no Vanguard finish completed; package `0.3.2` at `70` retained completed finishes in both difficulties. The automated threshold now requires a qualified finish-completion ratio so a start-only candidate cannot pass. No class win-rate target was used.

The historical v8 Chase screen separated outward control-return steering from the older recovery posture's tactical lock. Its original semantics left proactive launch available and suppressed only Duelist's auto-target Pressure Dash; an eight-frame candidate reduced some immediate re-launch counts but lowered durable reset quality and removed qualified Vanguard finish completion. `flow-v24` replaces those experimental semantics with the narrower bait/read behavior documented above, but its matched single and paired screens also failed. Reserve-aware natural-recovery reset candidates likewise reduced some Blocked Chase samples without passing finish and durable-reset gates. Steering and natural reset therefore remain zero-default designer controls; no result was promoted from a lower Chase count alone.

The v9 recovery-agency pass added a package multiplier for natural recovery reset and a mirrored **Human recovery agency** probe where P1, rather than the AI, regains control under a committed chase. On the fresh 12-set-per-direction Veteran matrix, a global reset scale of `0.75` reduced Chase from `10 Blocked / 40 Watch` to `4 Blocked / 46 Watch`, but added two Blocked Separation rounds and four Commitment Watch rounds. A global two-frame startup-clash window fixed the selected simultaneous-launch replay but created about 40 Blocked Exchange rounds per mirrored pairing; post-clash spacing did not rescue it. No default changed. The accepted result is a reproducible human and AI experiment path, not an inferred balance verdict.

The v10 observability pass did not alter controller policy. It added deterministic shared-agency telemetry so a distance exit is no longer assumed to be a decision: shared movement control excludes helpless, stun, and forced recovery; shared action-ready time additionally excludes end lag, parry, and active commitments; a shared decision window requires both action-ready fighters to remain outside pressure for `0.75s`. The current four-game mirrored sample found only `0.33` Cadet and `0.10` Veteran shared decision windows per round. Both fighters could steer for roughly `19%` of frames, but only `3.8-5.0%` of frames had both free to choose, and `81.5-85.2%` of steerable time remained in pressure. The new Commitment diagnostic flags this saturation and deliberately links to decision policy and per-character move families, not a false global collision lever. These values establish a baseline for the next matched experiment; they are not a target score and changed no live default.

The `flow-v15` commitment-reset screen first aligned the experimental timer with that action-ready definition, including parry, and stopped ordinary whiffs from repeatedly starting a bilateral reset. Fixed-seed `12`, `24`, and `48` reset-frame candidates and a `12`-frame observe-only candidate were still rejected. The longest reset reduced Blocked Chase samples but created widespread draws/timeouts and weaker Separation and exchange progression; observe-only moved the primary bottleneck to Separation with `21 Blocked / 29 Watch` samples. This rules out a broad cadence pause as the next live change. All three commitment values remain zero, and the next experiment must isolate a specific post-control attacker recommit without globally suppressing movement or the authored kit.

The `flow-v16` screen isolated that specific attacker transition with **Opponent Recovery Respect Frames**. It starts only when the opponent regains control, suppresses new offense while leaving defensive reactions available, and uses a mild orbit rather than a global retreat. On the same 12-set-per-direction Veteran matrix, baseline Commitment/Separation/Chase issue ratios were `92% / 20% / 100%`. Candidates at `12`, `18`, and `24` frames produced `100% / 34.6% / 100%`, `100% / 36% / 92%`, and `87.5% / 33.3% / 95.8%` respectively. The 12- and 18-frame candidates also added roughly nine to ten seconds to the average set; 24 frames stayed near baseline duration but introduced six Blocked Separation rounds. All were rejected and the new control remains zero. The result narrows the next question to the returning fighter's escape/defense choices and the physical reset created by a successful defensive event, rather than another attacker-wide cadence delay.

A later `flow-v25` minimum-boundary retest confirmed there is no safe positive range hidden below those earlier values. One respect frame reduced Blocked Chase from `12` to `6` on the current 48-round Cadet/Veteran screen, but it introduced six Blocked Commitment rounds, increased round timeouts from `8` to `14`, and reduced shared action-ready share in the affected Cadet matchup. The one-frame candidate was rejected and the shipped value remains zero.

Schema v10 adds **Post-Commitment Read Scale** as a narrower manual diagnostic. When an AI's launch, dunk, projectile, or command-grab commitment ends against a fully controllable opponent, the timer preserves the configured read until authored recovery has expired. Movement and emergency defense remain available, and the read is not armed against a helpless, stunned, or recovering target. A strict four-game-per-profile Cadet/Veteran screen changed only this value from `0` to `1`. Separation issue ratio improved from `13.64%` to `8.70%`, but Commitment gained four Blocked rounds and Chase gained two; Commitment issue ratio moved from `77.27%` to `78.26%`, Chase from `95.45%` to `95.65%`, and post-control reset ratios generally fell. The candidate was rejected and the shipped default remains zero.

`flow-v17` and schema v11 added **Tactical Reposition Weight** as an explicit alternative to a valid attack after control returns. The opportunity is armed once when the fighter regains control, expires if unused, never spends boost fuel, and cancels if a finish or immediate threat takes priority. A first broad neutral implementation was rejected immediately because repeated mutual orbiting produced drawn sets. The first narrowed screen exposed a cancellation defect: the movement posture ended for a threat or finish, but its decision and reaction locks remained. `flow-v18` releases both owned locks on cancellation and invalidates the older comparison evidence. On the fresh mirrored 12-set Cadet/Veteran matrix, the corrected `repositionWeightScale: 0 -> 1` candidate reduced Blocked Commitment from `14/102` to `2/100` and Blocked Chase from `16/102` to `8/100`; aggregate issue ratios still remained `70%` and `92%`, and Veteran Vanguard converted `0/7` qualified finish starts. A lower `0.5` weight restored only `1/9` Vanguard finishes and introduced Blocked Separation samples. Neither candidate is promotable. The zero-default **Post-control reposition V1** preset remains available only for direct human comparison.

These policies and designer values are AI evaluation tools, not class balance presets. Read timeout share, physical contact, pressure occupancy, shared movement control, action-ready share, sustained shared decision windows, spacing resets, carried brief re-entry, post-clash and post-exit action mix, ordinary-Boost reads, launch frequency, helpless share, finish cadence, and replayable flagged rounds together. Set win percentages remain context only. Policy- or behavior-changing reports deliberately receive different behavior fingerprints. Report schema v24 records a behavior-independent base AI fingerprint, versioned shared-agency and opponent-relative post-control telemetry, deterministic tactical-reposition, counterstep, chase-lock, repeat-dash, combat-boost-lock exposure counters, accepted ordinary-Boost counterplay grouped by defender, exact stage-scoped Blocked/Watch reason counts, and a fingerprinted exact rule snapshot. `--compare-report` requires one effective rule change by default and lists that value while still requiring the same controller revision, profile definitions, policies, seed matrix, packages, and simulation settings. Deliberate compound tests require `--allow-multi-rule-comparison` and remain visibly labelled. The neutral all-`legacy` path carries the current `flow-v25` controller identity; older controller revisions or pre-v15 reports are intentionally rejected as strict comparison baselines. Commitment reset frames count only while both fighters are action-ready, and only control returns or clashes start that shared reset. Ordinary offensive whiffs may transfer initiative but do not repeatedly force both fighters through a disengagement. Opponent recovery respect, tactical repositioning, between-exchange repositioning, and post-control counterstepping are independent zero-default controls. V22 reports remain valid historical baselines under their v9 telemetry provenance; unavailable v23 counterplay fields remain `N/A` rather than fabricated as zero.

The behavior-neutral v23 counterplay pass opens a bounded read only when a newly accepted ordinary Boost is within 48 units, closing on a collision path, and the defender was action-ready on the preceding frame. It records the defender's first later accepted action, any paid Super Boost during the window, movement intent, contact/pass state, interruption, and combat conversion without entering simulation state, AI decisions, rollback, or checksums. On the identical 48-round Cadet/Veteran mirrored baseline used for the rejected evasion prototype, all prior flow outcomes reproduced exactly: eight timeouts, Commitment `0 Blocked / 38 Watch / 10 Observed`, Separation `2 / 4 / 42`, Chase `12 / 36 / 0`, and 880 Super Boost starts. This does not prescribe a fix, but it confirms that current AI counterplay rarely turns an ordinary-Boost read into durable crossing or separation.
